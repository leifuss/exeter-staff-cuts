Modelling — redesign Exeter’s own pay spine

Every other tab asks how Exeter compares with another university. This one asks the next question: what should Exeter’s spine look like instead? Move the bands, and see what today’s workforce would cost. Figures and labels marked ⓘ carry a hover explainer.

What this does. It takes Exeter’s real 2023 staff distribution across the pay spine — every spine point from SP34 to SP71, 3,160 FTE — and re-prices it on whatever structure you build. The starting point is that real distribution priced on the 2026-27 scales; on 2025-26 scales the same calculation reproduces Exeter’s actual pay bill to the pound. Either way the baseline carries no modelling assumptions at all, so it is not in dispute.
What it is not. This is a steady state: what today’s workforce would cost if it had always been on the structure you build. It is therefore a long-run ceiling, not achievable savings, and it is not a transition plan — nobody’s pay can lawfully be cut, so a real change would bite only on new entrants and promotions (see the Where the saving comes from and Transition tabs). Read the assumptions at the foot before quoting anything.
Corrected, July 2026 — research-only staff. Switching the control below to exclude research-only staff uses per-band percentages derived from HESA research-only headcounts (555 FTE, 17.6% of this population). An earlier version of this site used HESA’s research-only share of academics instead (grade F −50%, G −10%), which applies an academics-only ratio to a population that also contains professional-services staff, who are never research-only, and removed about 336 FTE too many. The same correction has been applied to the Overview and the Where the saving comes from tab. Derivation and limits in the assumptions panel at the foot.
