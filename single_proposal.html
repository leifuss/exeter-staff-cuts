A single proposal — what the 2026 pay scale proposal would cost

One proposal, priced against Exeter’s current structure and against what Sheffield and Manchester already do — disaggregated by grade and by management.

A single proposal, for discussion. This tab prices the 2026 pay scale proposal (model 3) worked up in the joint-claim workbook. It keeps two reference points either side of it: the 2022 joint trade union claim, which is the only other real position anyone has taken, and the structures Sheffield and Manchester already operate. The two constructed straw models that used to sit here have been removed.
The proposal lengthens every grade from a lower floor: grade F runs SP31–36 instead of SP34–36, H runs SP46–51 instead of SP48–50, and Professorial Band 1 becomes automatic increments with Band 2 on annual review. Band 3 gains three increments at the bottom (SP65–71), in line with comparator practice. In plain terms that is lower entry pay in exchange for a longer guaranteed climb, and it is a pay cut for anyone near the bottom of a grade today.
The workbook now checks out. The scale is monotonic, headcount conserves to 0.02 FTE, every grade keeps its own establishment, the executive and Band 3 lines both compute at the redistribution’s own rate, and on-costs are carried through on the formula this site uses. The numbering below SP34 has been realigned to the published national spine, so “SP31” now means the published SP31 and the two documents can be read against each other directly.
Two points still differ from the published 2026-27 spine, both immaterial: SP51 reads £73,018 against a published £72,997 (£21 high, worth about £630 on the baseline across the 29.9 FTE there), and SP32 is £3.83 low. Neither changes any conclusion.
How this is priced. Each model is applied to Exeter’s real 2023 staff distribution across the pay spine, and every model is expressed as a map from each current spine point to a new one — which is exactly how the 2022 union claim itself is written. Switch to salary only and count all staff and the baseline is £181,014,710 across 3,159.8 FTE: Exeter’s real 2023 distribution priced on the 2026-27 scales that take effect on 1 August 2026. On the 2025-26 scales that same calculation reproduces Exeter’s published pay bill to the pound, so the baseline carries no modelling assumptions — only the pay award; the defaults above add employer on-costs and remove grant-funded research staff, so the figures read as a cost to the University. Because nothing here depends on modelling how fast people are promoted, the professorial figures carry none of the progression uncertainty that led us to retire the professorial cost ranking elsewhere on this site.
What it is not. A steady state: what today’s workforce would cost had it always been on each structure. Nobody’s pay can lawfully be cut, so a real change would bite on new entrants and promotions and phase in over a career. Read the assumptions at the foot before quoting anything.

One representative career, age 28 to 67, under each model

A single academic under six different structures — it shows what each proposal does to a career, which the annual totals below cannot. Dashed verticals mark promotions. Two routes are shown separately rather than averaged, because only a minority of academics become professors and a blended ‘average’ career describes nobody: the default is the majority route (grades F, G and H, then the remaining years at the Associate Professor ceiling), and the toggle switches to the professorial route. The familiar figure that professors are appointed at about 48 is an average of those who get there — it says nothing about how many do.

Years in each grade F G H Remaining years at the AP ceiling 20 — the career always ends at 67 Route Merit rate — biennial — annual Professorial progression is merit-based, and the two rates are set separately because the rounds are not comparable: a biennial round (Exeter today, and the 2022 claim) is a bigger step to clear than an annual one (the proposal’s Band 2; Sheffield and Manchester, after their automatic steps). Holding one rate across both would hand every annual-review structure twice the progression for free, so the annual rate defaults lower. At 40% biennial and 30% annual, a step takes about five years either way. Above each grade’s normal points, pay is the expected value of holding a contribution point (30%).
