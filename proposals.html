Proposals compared — what each pay model would cost

Six structures priced on the same workforce, against Exeter’s current structure as the baseline, disaggregated by grade and by management. The point is to make the trade-offs legible, not to advocate for any one of them: two of these models cost more and two are, in plain terms, pay cuts.

How this is priced. Each model is applied to Exeter’s real 2023 staff distribution across the pay spine, and every model is expressed as a map from each current spine point to a new one — which is exactly how the 2022 union claim itself is written. The baseline reproduces Exeter’s actual pay bill to the pound, and because nothing here depends on modelling how fast people are promoted, the professorial figures carry none of the progression uncertainty that led us to retire the professorial cost ranking elsewhere on this site.
What it is not. A steady state: what today’s workforce would cost had it always been on each structure. Nobody’s pay can lawfully be cut, so a real change would bite on new entrants and promotions and phase in over a career. Read the assumptions at the foot before quoting anything.
