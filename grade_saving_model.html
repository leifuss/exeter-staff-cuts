Where the saving comes from — grade by grade

One grade at a time. All universities sit on the same national pay spine, so a given spine point pays the same salary everywhere. Only the structure differs — which point a grade starts on, how many increments it contains, and what sits above its ceiling. This is deliberately simplified; it is here to make two principles concrete.

1. Exeter’s structure is expensive — and it is mostly about where each grade starts. Exeter’s Lecturer grade begins at spine point 34 (£43,482), the joint-highest in the Russell Group; most comparators begin between SP27 and SP32. The same pattern repeats at Senior Lecturer and Associate Professor. Because Exeter’s grades are also short (Lecturer has just 3 normal increments), staff reach the ceiling quickly and sit there. The columns below show that gap opening on day one and what it is worth across the workforce.
2. Nothing here re-bases anyone. The change bites only at the transition between grades. No current member of staff moves down a point and nobody’s pay is cut. A different structure applies only when someone enters a grade — whether recruited from outside or promoted in from the grade below. Everyone already in post keeps their salary and their increments. That is why the saving builds gradually, at roughly the rate staff move through the grades, rather than arriving in year one.
